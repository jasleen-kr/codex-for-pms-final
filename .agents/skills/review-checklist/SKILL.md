# Review checklist

Read the whole brief the user identifies, including any explicitly incorporated sections or attachments needed to assess it. If the brief is missing or inaccessible, ask for its location or text.

Check these four criteria:

| Criterion | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | The brief explicitly names an accountable person or clearly designated role that owns the work. A list of contributors or stakeholders alone is insufficient. |
| Success | It states the outcome sought and how it will be evaluated: a measurable target or observable acceptance condition, with a timeframe and measurement source where needed to judge success. Activities or deliverables alone do not demonstrate that the work succeeded. |
| Consistent scope | The problem, goals, proposed work, deliverables, success measures, and exclusions describe the same intended scope throughout. Flag unexplained additions, omissions, or contradictions in audience, use case, commitments, or boundaries. Clearly labeled future work is not a scope change. |
| Problem before fix | Before presenting the proposed solution, the brief explains who has the problem, what is going wrong, and why it matters. A short title or summary mentioning the solution is acceptable if the substantive explanation establishes the problem before developing the fix. |

Return a concise table with one row per criterion: **Pass**, **Needs clarification**, or **Missing**, supporting evidence with a section or line reference where available, and the smallest useful correction for any gap. Use Needs clarification for ambiguous or contradictory content; use Missing when required information is absent. Do not invent owners, targets, evidence, or scope to fill gaps.

Lead with whether the brief meets all four checks and identify the most important gaps. Keep findings within these criteria. Review only; edit the brief when the user requests edits.
