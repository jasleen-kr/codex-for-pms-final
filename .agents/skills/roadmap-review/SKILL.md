# Roadmap review

Read the roadmap or release proposal and relevant linked commitments. Check source dates and separate current evidence from inherited plans. An old roadmap entry or release number is not proof of an approved commitment.

Check:
- Initiatives have a problem/outcome, intended users, priority rationale and accountable owner.
- Status separates discovery, proposal, approved commitment and delivered work. Target dates/releases state assumptions and confidence; delivery claims have evidence.
- Sequence reflects dependencies, feasibility and capacity evidence. Identify unknown estimates without manufacturing precision.
- New work identifies potential displacement, affected customer promises and authority needed to change commitments.
- Validation, pilot, review and expansion gates specify evidence, decision owners and the response to insufficient evidence.
- Deferred and excluded work stays distinct from committed scope. Future discovery has a learning question and review trigger rather than an invented ship date.
- Cross-product dependencies and communication responsibilities are visible where affected.

For this course, commitment changes go through Helen/Product; Marcus is the engineering feasibility partner. Check Supply's availability dependency when relevant. Treat inherited 4.3 and Q4 plans as historical until a current source confirms their status.

Return a concise table of **Pass / Needs clarification / Missing**, evidence references and smallest corrections. Use Needs clarification for ambiguous, contradictory or unverified commitments; Missing for absent information. Explain justified inapplicable checks. Highlight conflicts and pending decisions. A proposed Now / Next / Later sequence can help, but must not become an implied promise.

Review only unless edits are requested. Document proposed changes in authorized project files without silently changing approved scope, dates or promises. Do not contact stakeholders or publish a roadmap.
